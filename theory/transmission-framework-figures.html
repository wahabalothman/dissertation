Transmission Framework: Theory and Methods by Stage

Working memo. How conspiratorial claims move from fringe origin through intermediaries to elite adoption, what "laundering" means operationally, how to build a cohort that includes failures, and how to keep the whole thing falsifiable without conflating online rhetoric with belief.

0. The claim, stated as something that can be wrong

Among conspiratorial allegations circulating on the fringe in a given window, the ones that reach elite political speech are disproportionately those that were transformed by an intermediary in three specific ways: the allegation survived, the attributed source was obscured, and the stigmatized wording was replaced. Obscuring the source raises the political acceptability of the claim more than it raises belief in it. Each clause is a separate test with a separate way to fail.

1. Fringe origin: enumerate, don't select

The methodological problem at stage 1 is not tracing; it is sampling. If the claim list comes from fact-checkers, prior literature, or elite speech traced backward, the cohort is selected on visibility or on the outcome. The fix is an enumeration rule applied to the fringe corpus before anything downstream is examined.

Process tracing is not an enumeration method; it is what you do to a handful of cases after the cohort exists.

The case definition (the part epidemiologists get right)

Epidemiology starts with a case definition that can be applied without knowing the outcome. Yours: a passage counts as a conspiratorial claim if it asserts that identifiable actors are secretly coordinating an action for a hidden purpose. Allegations of incompetence, ordinary rumor without covert coordination, and criticism of policy are excluded. Write it before coding; report how often coders disagree on the boundary.

"First observed appearance" is what the data give you. It is not authorship. Report it as first observed, and treat elite-first claims as part of the cohort rather than as errors.

"Laundering" is a metaphor used by Hemmer, Starbird (2017), and Keim et al. It needs to be broken into components that can vary independently, because they do.

Measuring "the allegation survived" with embeddings

Keim et al. vary time; you vary tier. Train one embedding space per large corpus (fringe; intermediary transcripts), align them, and compute their three distances for each claim family. Laundering predicts low CSD (meaning preserved) with low lexical overlap (vocabulary replaced) from fringe to intermediary. Stalled claims should not show this pattern. The elite corpus is too small for its own Word2Vec space; use sentence embeddings for claim matching there and reserve the diachronic metrics for the two large tiers.

That combination (HAD high, CSD low, LO low) is Keim's "semantic narrowing with lexical replacement." It is laundering measured in their terms. Run the Gonen et al. nearest-neighbor stability check because Procrustes alignment is seed-sensitive.

The cohort: the massive corpus question

You do not need a massive corpus of conspiracy theories. You need a bounded, complete cohort for one window, built by a rule, where most members fail. Think of it as a surveillance system for one region and one season, not a registry of every case ever.

Scale, honestly

  • Two weeks of /pol/ hand-read (pilot): 40 to 60 claim variants. Enough to see whether the pattern exists. Not enough for a model.
  • One election season, fringe + five shows + fixed elite list, LLM-extracted and validated: a few hundred variants and, realistically, 20 to 60 elite adoption events. That is a small event-history dataset; the model will be thin and the paired process tracing will carry weight.
  • Nothing here requires the 169M-comment scale of Keim et al. Their scale serves embedding stability on one platform over a decade. Your question is about one season across tiers.

3. Elite adoption: selection among available claims

Structure: claim × elite × week panel. Row exists while elite i has not yet adopted claim j. Outcome is first adoption in week t. Discrete-time event history (logit), elite and week fixed effects, standard errors clustered by claim. Week fixed effects absorb the shared news shocks that make Hawkes and Granger designs unreliable here.

Covariates lagged one to two weeks: fringe volume; intermediary coverage; whether a laundered version exists yet (A, B, C coded separately); prior adoption by other elites; compatibility with the elite's prior frames; issue salience. The laundering hypothesis is the coefficient on "source-obscured version exists" holding "allegation preserved" constant. Pair the model with four to six matched cases (adopted vs stalled, same family, similar prior visibility) coded for the three routes in Figure 1.

4. Electorate: acceptance is not belief, and rhetoric is not either

Two things the design refuses to conflate. First, online rhetoric is not public belief; the observational chapters measure what was said, by whom, and in what form, and make no claim about what audiences believe. Second, belief is not acceptance. The literature's baseline (roughly a tenth of Americans volunteer conspiracy talk unprompted; stable aggregate belief over time) is a scope condition, not an outcome to explain. The outcome at stage 4 is whether it becomes acceptable for a politician to advance a claim, and whether attributed origin moves that.

Experiment A: same allegation, 2×2 (original vs intermediary wording) × (earlier source hidden vs disclosed), politician and endorsement held constant. The estimand is the interaction. Experiment B: attributed authorship (citizens / campaign staff / unspecified) × politician's justification (responding to constituents / own judgment). Measure belief, political acceptance, sanction, investigation, and circulation separately; predefine an equivalence margin for belief; define skeptics by baseline belief. Pilot in a student pool; confirmatory via TESS or a quota sample.

The epidemiology analogy, used carefully

Tangherlini's earlier vaccine-narrative work was published in JMIR Public Health and Surveillance, so the analogy has precedent. It is useful for design discipline and misleading as a model of mechanism.

On training an LLM "to be an epidemiologist"

Don't train a model; write a protocol and have a model apply it. The useful part of the idea is the surveillance frame: a fixed case definition, fixed sources, fixed window, and a model that reads every passage and flags cases. That is exactly the LLM extraction step in stage 1, and open-weight models handle it. The part to drop is the disease-dynamics modeling (SIR, R0); it assumes passive, homogeneous spread, and your Figure 1 says spread here is selective, often deliberate, and sometimes carried by one person between two rooms. Keep the vocabulary of surveillance; leave the epidemic curves.

What to do first

  1. Write the case definition and the coding sheet (one page, no code).
  2. Hand-code two weeks of /pol/ from September 2024 with a second coder on a subsample; get a cohort of 40 to 60 variants and their fates. Report agreement.
  3. Run the provenance-cue search on Trump and Vance 2024 transcripts (test 0).
  4. Only if 1 and 2 show a pattern: build the extraction pipeline and the season-long cohort.
  5. Pilot the acceptance measures in Bolsen's pool in parallel; that is independent of everything above.
